# What I would tell myself after this pass

Study window 2017-01-01 to 2018-01-01. Household panel from the public Complete Journey mirrors: 2,469 households, 1,469,307 receipt lines, 155,848 baskets. Every figure below is in `exports/app/certified_kpis.json`, which is written from the marts.

## What moved

The retailer received **$4,596,039.58** in net sales. Recorded discounts were **$822,553.19**, which is **15.2%** of the pre-discount ring. About **49.4%** of those discount dollars sat on lines with no display and no mailer. A feature is not where most of the discount bill lives.

On product-store pairs with a real baseline (27,555 pairs, covering **39.1%** of non-fuel feature-week sales), counting silent weeks as zero, feature weeks added **$133,971.26** of net sales versus those pairs' own quiet weeks. That is a **61.0%** lift. The ROI proxy on that matched panel, incremental net sales divided by the discounts recorded on the feature weeks, is **0.78**. The extra cash did not cover the discount bill on those weeks.

That lift is mostly "a sale happened." On weeks that actually rang up, the average feature-week ring was **$3.48** against **$3.15** on a quiet week, a lift of **10.5%** (107,083 feature weeks, 303,347 quiet weeks). I cannot see which silent weeks were out of stock, so I quote both numbers and I do not lead with the larger one alone.

Largest department gaps on the matched panel:

- **GROCERY**: incremental $78,195.02, lift 54.7%, ROI proxy 0.80, 18267 pairs.
- **MEAT**: incremental $32,753.34, lift 134.8%, ROI proxy 0.84, 1547 pairs.
- **MEAT-PCKGD**: incremental $8,711.64, lift 55.1%, ROI proxy 0.67, 1465 pairs.
- **PRODUCE**: incremental $8,559.96, lift 41.0%, ROI proxy 0.54, 3787 pairs.
- **DELI**: incremental $2,725.04, lift 35.1%, ROI proxy 1.28, 867 pairs.

The category I would walk through is **DELI / DELI MEATS**. 470 product-store pairs, feature-week sales **$6,926.22**, quiet-week run-rate stretched over those weeks **$4,601.67**, gap **$2,324.55** (**50.5%**), discounts on those feature weeks **$1,107.22**.

## What did not

Campaign **8** (Type A, 2017-05-08 to 2017-06-25) is the one that does not pay. Incremental net sales **-$9,001.88** against coupon and match discounts of **$819.70** (ROI proxy **-10.98**). The mailed households handed over less cash than the comparison group. I trust the sign before I trust the dollar.

The largest positive gap was campaign **18** (Type A), incremental net sales **$17,943.99**, ROI proxy **9.13**. I would still want the spread across households in front of me before I treated that dollar as settled.

I scored **21** of **27** campaigns. The ones I skipped had too few mailed households or no pre-period inside the calendar. **11** scored campaigns have incremental net sales below zero, and **11** have an ROI proxy under 1. A few of those ratios look absurd because the coupon bill is a few dollars. I rank on the incremental dollars, not on a ratio with a tiny denominator.

## The forecast

The four-week mean beat the tree. Sales-weighted MAPE was **8.7%** for the mean and **13.1%** for the tree, so the default stays the mean. I would rather keep a forecast I can explain than one that loses to the recent average.

## Who responded

**26.4%** of households (653) put at least 18.0% of their pre-discount ring on a recorded discount, with at least 8 baskets. The **Champions** segment is 20.1% of households and 44.8% of net sales.

## What I would try next weekend

1. Take the worst scored campaign and read the pre-period by hand, household by household, before I believe the dollar. The sign is the part I trust first.
2. Spend the feature slots on the category in the worked example only if a quick week-level chart still shows the gap. Busy weeks can impersonate a lift.
3. Stop treating the loyalty discount as the cost of the mailer. Most of the discount bill is off feature. The campaign ROI I quote uses coupon and match discounts on the couponed products, and leaves the loyalty field beside it.
4. Keep the forecast default on whichever method won. If that is the four-week mean, I will not add model complexity until I have a second year.

## What I will not say

This was not a randomized test. The panel is frequent shoppers. There is no cost of goods, so none of these ROI figures are a margin. The language page, if I use it, is only allowed to repeat these tables.
